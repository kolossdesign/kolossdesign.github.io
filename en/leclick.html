At the time of the acquisition LeClick worked with more than 2,500 restaurants and bars in 25 Russian cities. The product became the basis of the booking service in Yandex.Eda.

What made this project different

  • A product from scratch: no legacy, no ready-made scenarios
  • A two-sided market – the guest and the restaurant want different things
  • The market validated the result: Yandex bought the product and built it into its ecosystem
  • I owned both the product side and the visual language

The task

Replace booking by phone with a scenario where the guest gets a confirmation instantly and the restaurant sees and controls its occupancy. In a two-sided product you cannot optimise for one side only: the guest needs speed, the restaurant needs a full room and predictability.

Discovery

I started with how booking works without a product: a phone call, the host’s notebook, manual checks. I looked into where time is lost and where no-shows come from.

I mapped the process from three sides at once – the guest, the host and the restaurant. Interviews with guests showed what they expect from a booking; interviews with restaurateurs showed what a full room means to them; watching a host’s shift and going through paper logbooks revealed what interviews never mention: the real mechanics of rush hour.

Key findings

  • The guest does not need a booking, they need a guarantee. As long as the confirmation exists only in words, the guest treats the table as “sort of booked” and calmly keeps looking at other options.
  • Demand is lost before the conversation even starts. Some guests never get through during rush hour – exactly when a booking is most valuable to the restaurant. Nobody counts those people.
  • The host keeps the floor plan in their head. The seating plan is not recorded anywhere, so the same table is easily promised twice and the knowledge does not survive a shift change.
  • A no-show is not a problem until it becomes visible. Without data the restaurant cannot tell a no-show from a cancellation and cannot do anything about it.
  • What matters to the restaurant is predictability, not the number of bookings. The value of the product for a venue is a clear picture of the evening ahead, not an individual request.

The solution

The guest books and gets a confirmation instantly. The restaurant sees its occupancy and manages seating without manual checks. Two environments: the site to choose, the app to book on the go.

Desktop: finding and choosing a restaurant

The list and the map are linked: the guest filters venues and the restaurant card with its slots opens in a side sheet – without leaving the results.

App: booking on the go

The same scenario in a few taps: restaurants nearby, a time and a table, confirmation and booking history in the profile.

What this experience taught me

A tool built around food has to stay easy to control – for the guest and for the floor. The winner is not the one who made it convenient for the guest, but the one who made it convenient for the guest and the restaurant alike.
