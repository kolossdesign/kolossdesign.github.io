Designed a restaurant SaaS product from scratch

Role: Product Designer

Scope: visual language, design system, booking by calendar and floor plan, venue onboarding and the dashboard.

Stages: Concept • Visual language • Design system • UX/UI • Delivery

A new startup: SaaS for restaurant automation. The product design was built from scratch – from the visual language to the floor-level scenarios. The project is currently in development.

What makes this project different

  • A product from scratch: no legacy, no ready-made scenarios, no accumulated compromises
  • A working tool, not a showcase: the interface is used throughout the shift
  • Input speed matters more than looks – every order is counted in seconds
  • Different roles in one product: host, waiter, manager

The task

Build a modern, convenient and intuitive SaaS tool for restaurants. Not a redesign of something existing, but a product a new employee can figure out without training and an experienced one can use faster than a paper notebook.

The design system came first

I started with the system, not the screens: tokens, grid, typography and a component library with states. The product grows fast, and without a shared foundation the screens would have drifted apart by the second iteration. Engineering got a ready toolkit instead of a pile of images.

Approach and principles

I started from a real shift rather than a feature list: I broke down what the floor work consists of, where waiting and mistakes appear, and built the screens around that. The principles I put at the core:

  • Core actions in one tap, no nested menus
  • Large tap targets and readability in side light and in a hurry
  • The interface suggests the next step instead of relying on training and manuals
  • Each role sees only its own scenarios: a waiter never drowns in reports
  • One design system from day one so the product can grow without drift
